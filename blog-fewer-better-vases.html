A slow case for buying fewer, better vases

What changes when you stop collecting and start choosing — plus the two we actually kept.

There was a stretch where every trip to a home store meant coming back with another vase — cheap enough to not think twice about, different enough from the last one to feel like a new idea. The shelf filled up fast. What didn't happen was any of them becoming the one we reached for. They just sat there, interchangeable, none of them quite right.

Eventually we boxed most of it up and started over with a different rule: buy one at a time, live with it for a season, and only replace it if it's actually earned a reason to go. Two years later, we're down to two vases. Both get used weekly. Here's what made the cut, and why fewer turned out to matter more than which ones.

1. The everyday shape

Best for weekly flowers

Hand-Thrown Ceramic Vase, Speckled Stoneware

This is the one that gets used every single week, which says more than any material or finish could. The wide mouth means it doesn't fight you when you're arranging whatever's on hand — grocery store tulips, garden cuttings, a single stem on a slow week. The weighted base means it doesn't tip when the stems lean. Nothing about it demands attention, which is exactly why it's still on the shelf.

What we let go of

Nearly everything under a certain price point, honestly. Not because cheap vases can't be good — but because we noticed a pattern: the ones we stopped using were always the ones we'd bought without a specific reason, just because they were inexpensive and available. The two that stayed both got bought to solve an actual, specific problem on the shelf.

If there's a rule in this, it's that one: buy for a gap you can name, not for a display. Everything else tends to become clutter with better lighting.
